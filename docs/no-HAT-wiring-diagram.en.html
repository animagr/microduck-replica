Right after charging it runs right at the servo's absolute maximum. Only when the voltage sags under load to 6.6–8.2 V is it back in the safe zone. Do not do stall tests on a full battery; for long-term storage, store at 3.8 V/cell.
Feetech pin order is the exact reverse of Dynamixel
Feetech AMP2.0-3P  1=Signal  2=Vcc  3=GND
DXL  JST EH        1=GND     2=Vcc  3=Signal
The two have different pitches (2.0 vs 2.5 mm) and will not fit into each other — so mis-plugging is physically prevented, and the only remaining risk is crimping your own cable backwards. After crimping, check every wire with a multimeter before powering on.

⚠ Three ways to burn things
1Power the adapter board logic from 3.3V only (pin 1). On 5V, its TX drives 5V levels into the controller's 3.3V RX pin. 25V fed into the 40-pin header is unprotected: it bypasses the board's own input protection, and reversed polarity kills the board instantly. Measure polarity and voltage before plugging in. 3Do not use Dupont wires for the servo main line. 15 servos peak at several amps; 24–28AWG Dupont wire only handles about 1A and will get hot or even melt its insulation.
What you lose without the HAT
· Onboard audio (codec + amplifier + mic)
· Shutdown detect signal LM5050-1 · IO_06
· Pi HAT+ identification (the EEPROM is not populated anyway)
· Onboard BMI088 (the official software does not use it anyway)
No shutdown signal + microSD = hard power cuts easily corrupt the filesystem. Shut down in software first, then cut power.
